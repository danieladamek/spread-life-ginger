## What it is
The European Medicines Agency's herbal committee accepts a use either on published clinical evidence (well-established use) or on long-standing use alone (traditional use). For ginger it accepts one well-established use, powdered rhizome for preventing motion sickness in adults, and lists traditional uses including stomach complaints, loss of appetite and the common cold [7]. It advises, as a precaution, that use in pregnancy is preferably avoided, even though a cohort of more than a thousand exposed pregnancies found no excess of malformations [6, 7].

## How this guide uses it
Where the monograph and the trials differ, the guide reports both. A regulator's acceptance is a judgement about the evidence for a product used as directed, not a recommendation to anyone; this guide gives no dosing advice. <!-- framing -->
