## What it is
In humoral medicine health was a balance of four humours, each hot or cold and wet or dry. Ginger was classed as hot and dry and used to correct a cold, wet constitution and diseases attributed to cold [51]. Texts graded the quality by degree: an Irish materia medica rates ginger hot in the third degree and wet in the second [52], and Gerard's 1597 herbal hot and dry in the third degree [53]. The same reasoning set who should avoid it: a 1745 treatise judged it good for the old and phlegmatic and harmful to the young of hot, bilious temperament [55].

## How this guide uses it
The humoral grading explains why ginger appears in old remedies for the stomach and for chills. It is a theory of its time, not evidence that a remedy works; the guide sets each historical claim beside what the trials show. <!-- framing -->
