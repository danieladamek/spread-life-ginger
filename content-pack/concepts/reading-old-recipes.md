## What it is
Old recipes often say which form of ginger to use, and the form matters. The fourteenth-century sauce gyngener stirs powdered, dried ginger cold into vinegar [152]; dried ginger carries shogaols as well as gingerols [17]. Boiling in water shifts gingerol toward shogaol, and mild acidity is where gingerol is most stable [30]. Names can mislead: a fifteenth-century gyngerbrede has no ginger in it [161], and the 1796 molasses gingerbread has none either [159].

## How this guide uses it
Each recipe records its date, place, source, the form of ginger and whether it was heated, then a short science note. Medicinal recipes are shown as historical documents, not advice, and no quantity or step is added to what the source says. <!-- framing -->
