## What it is
Ginger swells its rhizome when days shorten; long days favour leafy growth [22]. It can be grown without soil: a Florida greenhouse hydroponic system out-yielded field growing [146], and in a Malaysian fertigation trial plants did best in coir dust [147]. Because ginger is propagated from rhizome pieces, disease travels with the planting stock; tissue-cultured plants keep the chemistry of conventionally grown ones [18]. Soft rot from Pythium [138] and bacterial wilt from Ralstonia, now recorded as far north as Minnesota [149], are the main threats.

Section 14 sets out what the studies tested and where their conditions differ from a university greenhouse. It gives no growing prescription beyond what the cited studies report. <!-- framing -->
