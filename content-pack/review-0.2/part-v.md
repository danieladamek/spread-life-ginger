# Part V. Joining the records

<!-- section: 15-from-rhizome-to-kitchen -->
## 15. From rhizome to kitchen

What a preparation delivers depends on what was done to the rhizome before anyone ate it. This is where the chemistry of section 3 meets the recipes and the trials. <!-- framing -->

### Heat, water and time

Fresh rhizome extracted at room temperature contains gingerols and no detectable shogaols [@wohlmuth2005]; shogaol and zingerone are products of heat or alkali, not natural constituents [@connell1969]. In water, 6-gingerol and 6-shogaol interconvert reversibly; at 100 °C and pH 1 the reaction reached equilibrium within two hours, and the compounds were most stable at pH 4 [@bhattarai2001]. Moist heat converts gingerols to shogaols more efficiently than dry heat: moist heat at 120 °C for six hours gave the highest yield in one study, 2991 mg of 6-shogaol per kilogram of ginger [@jung2017]. Hot-air drying at 120 to 180 °C likewise lowered gingerols and raised shogaols in step with temperature and time [@ghasemzadeh2018]. Gentler sun-drying keeps more of the volatile oil [@ghasemzadeh2018].

<!-- figure: fig-drying -->

### What this means for a cup of tea

No study read for this review measures what a household infusion of fresh ginger extracts. Two findings bear on it. Commercial ginger teas, supplements and beverages vary widely in their gingerol and shogaol content [@schwertner2007], and a mild acidity is where 6-gingerol is most stable [@bhattarai2001]. A tea of fresh rhizome steeped briefly in hot water should therefore be a gingerol drink with little shogaol, while long boiling, drying the rhizome first or adding acid would shift the balance; that is an inference from the chemistry, not a measurement. <!-- synthesis --> <!-- framing -->

### The enzyme

Fresh ginger also carries zingibain, a protease that digests collagen readily, works best near 60 °C and is destroyed at about 70 °C [@thompson1973]. The same activity clots milk [@huang2011]. Fresh ginger in a marinade, or juice stirred into warm milk, is doing enzymatic work that dried ginger or a boiling pot cannot. <!-- synthesis -->

### Reading the old recipes through the chemistry

Historical recipes specify the form of ginger more often than one might expect, and the form matters. The fourteenth-century English ginger sauce calls for powdered ginger stirred cold into vinegar [@formeofcury1780]: dried material, and so on the chemical evidence a source of shogaols as well as gingerols. Medieval hippocras was a spiced wine made from a powder of cinnamon, white Mecca ginger, grains of paradise, nutmeg and galingale [@pichon1846]. The ginger beers of the nineteenth century boiled bruised dried ginger with sugar and fermented it [@randolph1830; @beeton1869]; the Chinese and Indian domestic remedies used the juice of the fresh rhizome [@dymock1893]; and the Chinese pharmacopoeia kept fresh and dried ginger as separate drugs [@stuart1911]. Seen this way, the traditional distinction between fresh and dried ginger and the modern chemistry of gingerol and shogaol describe the same fact from two directions. <!-- synthesis -->

Most clinical trials gave dried, powdered ginger in capsules, and some gave standardised extracts [@crichton2022]. Neither tells us directly about the fresh-ginger tea the greenhouse serves. <!-- synthesis --> <!-- framing -->

### The recipe collection

The recipes section of this guide gathers thirty-two preparations from dated sources, from Roman spiced salts [@apicius1922] to a Chinese American pickle of 1914 [@bosse1914]. Ginger beer travelled furthest: much the same drink of boiling water, sugar, an acid and ginger appears in Virginia in 1824 [@randolph1830], in Madras in 1850, started with palm toddy in place of brewer's yeast [@riddell1850], and in London in 1861 [@beeton1869]. Gingerbread moved the other way: Hannah Glasse's gingerbreads of the 1740s put beaten ginger first among the spices with treacle and butter [@glasse1751], Eliza Acton's of 1845 is leavened with carbonate of soda [@acton1845], and American versions became molasses cakes in which ginger is one spice among several [@simmons1796; @fisher1881]. The medicinal recipes run alongside: Ayurvedic spice powders and confections built on dried ginger [@dutt1877], and English herbal practice adding ginger to purges to settle the stomach [@culpeper1835]. <!-- synthesis -->

### A note on gingerbread

"Gingerbread" did not always contain ginger. A fifteenth-century English recipe for *gyngerbrede* is a honey-and-breadcrumb confection spiced with saffron, pepper and cinnamon, with no ginger at all [@austin1888]; an American molasses gingerbread of 1796 likewise names cinnamon and coriander or allspice and no ginger, while the same book's "soft gingerbread" uses four ounces [@simmons1796]. <!-- framing -->

<!-- section: 16-methods-of-the-field -->
## 16. Methods of the field

Each record has characteristic weaknesses, and knowing them is most of what is needed to read this literature well. <!-- framing -->

### Trials and reviews

Clinical evidence on ginger is abundant and uneven. Of 109 randomised trials reviewed in 2020, 39.4% were judged to provide high-quality evidence [@anh2020]. Reviews of reviews now exist in number: one counted 27 systematic reviews in 2019 and found their quality heterogeneous and only one reporting the funding of its included studies [@li2019]; another, in 2022, covered 24 reviews with only 3% overlap of primary studies [@crichton2022]; a third found 49% overlap among seven meta-analyses of pregnancy nausea [@tiani2024]. A large count of meta-analyses is not a large count of independent experiments. The test article is the weakest point: products vary several-fold in their gingerol content [@schwertner2007], and the umbrella review notes poor reporting of what was given [@crichton2022].

### Laboratory and animal work

Mechanistic work uses pure compounds at set concentrations in cells and isolated tissue [@walstab2013; @pertz2011], while human pharmacokinetics finds the compounds circulating as conjugates and metabolites [@zick2008; @zhang2022]. An effect at a concentration that is never reached in blood is a lead, not an explanation.

### Plant science

Function is usually inferred from gene expression and from laboratory inhibition tests [@li2021; @ficker2003]. The 2026 demonstration of a gingerol-pathway enzyme in bacteria [@pena2026] shows the standard of evidence that pathway claims can now meet.

### The historical record

The documentary record is selective: it preserves what was written down by physicians, merchants and cooks, mostly in Europe, China and India. Secondary statements about ginger's history in biomedical papers often repeat one another without a primary source, as with its origin (section 4). Nineteenth-century scholarly compilations such as *Pharmacographia* [@flueckiger1879] remain the best guides to the primary texts and are cited here for that reason.

<!-- section: 17-open-questions -->
## 17. Open questions

These are the points on which the sources read disagree or fall silent. <!-- framing -->

- **Vomiting in pregnancy.** Single trials found both nausea and vomiting reduced [@vutyavanich2001]; pooled analyses find only nausea [@viljoen2014].
- **Chemotherapy.** Positive [@pillai2010; @ryan2012], negative [@zick2008b] and mixed [@crichton2023; @choi2022] results remain unreconciled.
- **Motion sickness.** A positive first study [@mowrey1982] and a positive sea trial [@grontved1988] against several negative laboratory studies [@wood1988; @stewart1991], yet accepted by the European regulator [@emamonograph2025].
- **Lipids.** Two meta-analyses of similar size disagree [@zhu2018; @ebrahimzadeh2022].
- **Safety in pregnancy.** No harm detected in a cohort of more than a thousand exposed women [@heitmann2012]; regulators still advise avoidance as a precaution [@emamonograph2025].
- **Anticoagulants.** Reassuring small studies [@jiang2005; @lumb1994] against one signal in patients [@shalansky2007].
- **The active molecule.** Free gingerols are not found in blood after oral ginger [@zick2008].
- **The rest of the pathway.** The key synthase is now known [@pena2026]; the reductions that complete gingerol are not yet shown.
- **What gingerols do for the plant.** Untested directly [@dang2023; @ficker2003].
- **Where ginger was domesticated.** Asserted in several forms, demonstrated in none [@kizhakkayil2011; @cheng2021].

<!-- section: 18-how-to-read-this-literature -->
## 18. How to read this literature

A few habits will save a reader of the ginger literature a good deal of time. <!-- framing -->

Ask first what was tested: fresh rhizome, dried powder, a two-species extract [@altman2001] or a standardised one [@crichton2023] are different materials, and fresh and dried ginger differ chemically [@wohlmuth2005]. Ask what was measured: severity or incidence, nausea or vomiting, a symptom or a laboratory value. Prefer the umbrella reviews and the regulator's monograph for orientation [@crichton2022; @emamonograph2025], and go to the individual trials before repeating a number. Treat any statement about ginger's history in a biomedical paper as a lead to be checked against the primary record. And where a cell-culture result is offered as an explanation of a clinical one, recall that the compound may not reach the tissue in that form [@zick2008]. <!-- synthesis --> <!-- framing -->
